# NRICH primary problems - phrasing and pedagogy digest

Sources: 24 sampled problems from the NRICH primary curriculum map (stage 1-2).
https://nrich.maths.org/curriculum-maps/primary

## Structure worth borrowing
- Every problem ships with: **Problem** (short, inviting, often one question), **Getting Started** (a hint that narrows without solving: "Try looking at the top of the dominoes first, then the bottom."), **Key questions** (teacher prompts), **Why do this problem?** (pedagogical purpose), **Possible extension/support**.
- This is exactly the shape a tutor needs: problem -> graded hint -> probing questions -> extension. The "Getting Started" hint is the model for a first-level hint that does not give away the answer.

## Voice and phrasing
- Problems address the child directly, second person: "What comes next in each pattern of dominoes?"
- Low threshold, high ceiling: anyone can start, nobody finishes quickly.
- "Always, Sometimes or Never" statements for conjecture-evaluation ("Adding two odd numbers gives an even number - always, sometimes or never true?") - a reusable item type for reasoning.
- Student solutions from real children are published as models - shows the expected register of a child's explanation.

## Key questions (verbatim)
- "Which angles are important?"
- "What would change if that angle was bigger/smaller?"
- "How many ways can you find of doing it? How do you know you have found them all?" (systematic-working prompt)

## Extraction notes
- 24 problems sampled evenly across the 377-problem map; problem statements + key questions extracted (96 items). Full teacher text (why/approach/extension) is on the source pages, not duplicated into items.
